## A

**Ancillary**  
Secondary expenses that support a project but are not part of the main scope, such as permits, delivery fees, or small incidental items.

**Asynchronous Operating Environment**  
A workflow system where tasks, communication, and decision-making occur independently of real-time interaction, allowing people and processes to progress on their own schedules without needing simultaneous participation.

## B

**Backlog**  
The total value of contracted work a company has secured but not yet completed or billed.

**Bid Meeting**  
A scheduled discussion between the project team and stakeholders to review scope, pricing, assumptions, risks, and readiness before submitting a final bid.

**Bid Package**  
A bundle of drawings, specs, addenda, schedules, and instructions used to prepare a formal price.

**Bid Price**  
The total amount a contractor proposes to complete a project, including direct and indirect costs, overhead, profit, and taxes.

**Bid Spread**  
Comparing multiple bids to evaluate competitiveness, scope coverage, and red flags.

## C

**Capabilities Statement**  
A concise document that highlights a company's core competencies, past performance, differentiators, and key information to present its qualifications for government or commercial contracting opportunities.

**Cash Flow Statement**  
A financial report showing how money moves into and out of a business over a specific period, revealing liquidity, operational health, and the company's ability to meet obligations.

**Change Order (CO)**  
A formal modification to contract price, schedule, or scope after the project has started.

**Construction Manager as Agent (CMAA/CM-Agency)**  
A delivery method where the construction manager acts solely as the owner's representative without taking on financial risk, providing advisory and management services throughout the project.

**Construction Manager at Risk (CMAR)**  
A delivery method where the construction manager commits to delivering the project within a Guaranteed Maximum Price (GMP) while providing preconstruction services and overseeing construction.

**Content Management System (CMS)**  
A software platform that allows users to create, edit, organize, and publish website content without needing to write code.

**Cost Code**  
A numeric tracking system for categorizing expenses (materials, labor, equipment, overhead).

**Cost-Plus Contract**  
A contract where the owner reimburses the contractor for actual project costs and pays an additional fee or percentage for profit.

**Critical Path**  
The sequence of tasks that directly determines the project's finish date.

**Customer Relationship Management (CRM)**  
A system or platform used to manage interactions with leads and customers, track sales activity, automate communication, and strengthen long-term relationships.

## D

**Davis-Bacon Act**  
A federal law requiring contractors and subcontractors on publicly funded construction projects to pay workers no less than the locally prevailing wages and benefits as determined by the U.S. Department of Labor.

**Design-Bid-Build (DBB)**  
A traditional project delivery method where the owner hires a designer to create plans, then solicits competitive bids from contractors to build the project.

**Design-Build (DB) Contract**  
A contract where one entity is responsible for both designing and constructing the project, streamlining communication, and reducing coordination risk.

**Direct Costs**  
Expenses tied directly to scope of work, including materials, equipment, subcontractors, direct labor, and other.

## E

**Employee Stock Option Program (ESOP)**  
A benefit plan that gives employees the opportunity to earn or purchase company ownership shares, aligning their long-term incentives with the organization's performance.

**Equipment Rate**  
The hourly or daily cost of running a machine, including fuel, maintenance, and operator.

**Estimate vs. Actuals Report**  
A comparison between estimated costs and actual job costs.

**Estimator's Log**  
A day-by-day record of questions, clarifications, assumptions, and data sources used during the estimate.

## F

**File-Mapping**  
The organized structuring and naming of digital folders and files so information can be easily found, accessed, and maintained across a project or business workflow.

## G

**General & Administrative (G&A)**  
Costs and indirect business expenses, like office staff, rent, insurance, and software, that support the company but which are not tied to any specific project.

**General Conditions**  
Indirect project costs required to run the jobsite, including PM time, temporary power, dumpsters, fencing, toilets, and supervision.

**Go/No-Go Process**  
A structured decision-making step used to determine whether a project, bid, or opportunity is worth pursuing based on risk, capacity, profitability, and strategic alignment.

**Guaranteed Maximum Price (GMP) Contract**  
A cost-plus contract with a cap, where the contractor is reimbursed for actual costs but cannot exceed an agreed-upon maximum price unless changes are approved.

## H

**Hit Ratio**  
The percentage of awarded bids out of all bids submitted, your estimator's "batting average."

**Human Balance Sheet**  
A personal inventory of your assets and liabilities, including skills, habits, health, relationships, and mindset, to evaluate your overall capacity, resilience, and long-term growth potential.

## I

**Indefinite Delivery Indefinite Quantity (IDIQ)**  
A flexible federal contract that provides an undefined quantity of services or construction over a set period, with work issued through task orders.

**Indirect Cost**  
Costs not directly tied to labor or materials, such as equipment insurance, field vehicles, and estimator salaries.

**Inspection Readiness**  
Ensuring phases of work meet building code and are prepared for official review before covering or progressing.

**Integrated Project Delivery (IPD)**  
A collaborative contract model where the owner, designer, and contractor share risks, responsibilities, and rewards to improve efficiency and reduce waste.

## J

**Job-Costing**  
The process of tracking and assigning all project expenses, materials, labor, equipment, and overhead, to determine actual costs and profitability.

**Job Order Contracting (JOC)**  
A contract method used for small, repetitive, or fast-turnaround projects where pricing is based on preset unit costs from a published construction task catalog.

## L

**Labor Burden**  
The true cost of employing labor, includes workers comp, payroll taxes, benefits, and insurance.

**Lifestyle Medicine**  
A health approach that prevents, treats, and often reverses chronic disease through evidence-based lifestyle changes such as nutrition, movement, sleep, stress management, and social connection.

**Long Lead Item**  
Products that require extended ordering time (e.g., electrical panels, storefront doors, roof trusses).

**Lowest Price Technically Acceptable (LPTA)**  
A procurement method where the contract is awarded to the lowest-priced bid that meets all minimum technical and performance requirements.

**Lump Sum (Fixed Price) Contract**  
A contract where the contractor agrees to complete the entire project for one fixed total price regardless of actual costs.

## M

**Master Takeoff**  
The comprehensive measurement of all MESLO formulas for the scope of service or building the product.

**Mentor-Protege**  
A structured relationship where an experienced professional (mentor) guides, trains, and supports a less-experienced individual or firm (protege) to accelerate growth, skills, and capability.

**MESLO**  
A structured estimating system that integrates Materials, Equipment, Subcontractors, Labor, Other Costs, Insurance/Bond, Overhead, Profit, and Taxes to minimize risk and maximize profits.

## N

**Nonresponsive Bid**  
A proposal that fails to meet bid requirements and is therefore automatically disqualified.

## O

**Operationalizing Back Office**  
The process of systemizing and streamlining administrative functions, such as billing, scheduling, payroll, and documentation, to improve efficiency, accuracy, and overall business performance.

**Overage/Waste (O/W)**  
A planned percentage added to MESLO formula to account for expected waste, cuts, damage, or installation inefficiencies during construction based on complexity.

**Overhead (OH)**  
Business costs that exist regardless of project size or number of jobs, including office rent, company vehicles, admin salaries, HR, and marketing.

**Owner's Representative (Owner's Rep)**  
A professional acting on behalf of the owner to oversee design, construction, budget, and schedule.

## P

**Permanent Delegation**  
Assigning a recurring responsibility or decision-making authority to someone else long term so the task no longer requires the leader's direct involvement.

**Product-Pricing Strategy**  
A structured approach to determining a product's selling price based on production costs, market conditions, customer value, and profit goals.

**Productivity Rate**  
A measurement of how much work labor can complete within a specific time frame (e.g., 120 sq. ft. of drywall per hour).

**Profit & Loss (P&L) Reports by Class and Job**  
Financial statements that break down revenue and expenses by specific categories or individual projects, allowing a company to measure performance, profitability, and cost control at a granular level.

**Progressive Design-Build**  
A two-phase design-build contract where the contractor and designer are selected early based on qualifications and work collaboratively with the owner before finalizing the price.

**Project Labor Agreement (PLA)**  
A prehire collective bargaining agreement that sets wages, work rules, dispute-resolution procedures, and labor conditions for all contractors and subcontractors on a specific construction project.

**Prompt Payment Act**  
A law that requires federal agencies to pay contractors and vendors on time, typically within thirty days of receiving a proper invoice, or pay interest penalties for delays.

**Public-Private Partnership (P3)**  
A long-term contract where a private entity finances, designs, builds, operates, or maintains a public project in exchange for defined revenue or performance-based returns.

**Punch List**  
A list of deficiencies or incomplete work required before final payment and project closeout.

## Q

**Quality Assurance/Quality Control**  
Quality assurance (QA) focuses on preventing defects and ensuring that processes meet quality standards, while quality control (QC) involves inspecting and testing products to identify and correct defects. Together, they form a comprehensive quality management system that enhances product reliability and customer satisfaction.

## R

**Resource Allocation**  
The strategic assignment of labor, materials, equipment, and time to ensure a project is completed efficiently and on schedule.

**Retainage**  
A portion of the contract payment withheld by the owner until the project or specific milestones are satisfactorily completed to ensure performance and quality.

**RFI (Request for Information)**  
A formal question to the architect or engineer to clarify missing or conflicting information.

**Risk Factor**  
A condition or variable that increases the likelihood of cost overruns, delays, or performance issues on a project.

## S

**Schedule of Values (SOV)**  
A detailed breakdown of the contract amount by scope category used for monthly billing.

**Service-Pricing Strategy**  
A deliberate method for setting the price of services by balancing costs, market demand, value provided, and desired profit.

**Site Visit**  
An in-person inspection of the project location to understand existing conditions, verify measurements, identify risks, and confirm scope before estimating or construction begins.

**Submittals**  
Material samples, product data, or shop drawings submitted for approval before installation.

## T

**Time and Materials (T&M) Contract**  
A contract where the owner pays for the actual labor hours and materials used, plus an agreed-upon markup or fee.

**Trades Breakdown**  
A structured list of the subcontractor trades required to complete the project (e.g., demo, framing, drywall, paint, HVAC, electrical, plumbing).

## U

**Unit Cost**  
The cost per individual unit (per SF, LF, CY, EA) used to calculate total project cost.

**Unit Price Contract**  
A contract where the contractor is paid a fixed rate per measurable unit of work, such as per linear foot, square foot, or cubic yard, based on actual completed quantities.

## V

**Value Engineering (VE)**  
Evaluating lower-cost alternatives that maintain function, quality, or safety.

**Value of bid price (x)**  
The total monetary value assigned to completing a project, representing all costs, overhead, and profit included in the final bid.

**Value of time (t)**  
The measurable worth of how long a project or task takes, representing schedule impact, efficiency, and opportunity cost.

## W

**WHOIS Privacy**  
A domain protection service that hides a website owner's personal contact information from the public WHOIS database to prevent spam, data-scraping, and unwanted solicitations.

**Work In Progress (WIP)**  
A financial snapshot of active jobs, billing status, over/under billing, and earned value against schedule.
